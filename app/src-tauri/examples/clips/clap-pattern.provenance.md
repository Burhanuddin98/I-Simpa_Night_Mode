# clap-pattern.wav
- Title: Claps and rim clicks with silences (generated by this repository's code, not recorded)
- Made by: tools/clips/make_dry_clips.py (`python tools/clips/make_dry_clips.py`; `--check` regenerates and compares byte for byte), seed 0x4e4d44525931 through numpy's PCG64, numpy 2.5.2; the three clips are drawn from one generator in the order clap-pattern, pluck-melody, drum-groove
- Licence: GPL-3.0-only, as the app (the script and what it writes are this repository's own work)
- Source page: none; synthesised, nothing was recorded or fetched
- sha256: 478071895c2733414f017204aedddb2237b4b0bc69df8e73459878cb7175fb3c
- Format: 12.000 s, 48 kHz, mono, 16-bit PCM, peak-normalised to -1.0 dBFS (gain 0.365184 on the synthesised samples), no dither
- What it is: 8 hand-clap-like bursts and 5 rim clicks, silences of 0.50 to 1.45 s between one event's last sample and the next onset. A clap: three noise flicks 2-4 ms apart and a main burst decaying with a 6-8 ms time constant, noise band-passed about 600 Hz to 6 kHz (zero-phase, by FFT), 32 ms long before its release. A rim click: four damped modes near 0.75, 1.65, 2.9 and 4.4 kHz (time constants 6, 4, 3, 2 ms) over a 1 ms noise transient, 18 ms before its release. Levels: claps 0 to -4 dB, rim clicks -5 to -7 dB, random per event.
- Each event's end (its release): damped by 60 dB in 35 ms (exponentially) and faded to exact zero by 40 ms (raised cosine).
- Dryness: anechoic by construction (no room, no microphone, no reverberation in the synthesis). Measured on this file as written (read back): for each of its 13 events, the time from its end until every 1 ms frame up to the next event is 60 dB or more below the mean square of the millisecond before the end: at most 22 ms, median 19 ms (criterion: within 50 ms). After every event the file is digital zero until the next onset: those silences last 0.520 to 1.468 s.
