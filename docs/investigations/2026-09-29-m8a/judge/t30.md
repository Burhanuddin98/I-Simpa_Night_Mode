# M8a judge: T30, recomputed independently

**Verdict: PASS.** I recomputed T30 from the solver's raw receiver files for all 40 cells: 400 runs and
7,648 seed-receiver-band values, 6,240 of them in the 32 gated cells. My T30 gives the same checks A, B
and C as `report.json` to within 6.4 × 10⁻⁵, where the limits are 0.02 to 0.05. No verdict changes, and
no difference comes near a limit. There are no blocking or major findings.

Bed: `C:\tmp\nm-m8a-bed\20260929T093134Z` (build `da6f15c`). Script: `t30_recompute.py` beside this
file (one process, about 16 s). It writes its full output, per value and per cell, to
`t30_recompute.json`.

## 2. Method (ISO 3382-1; the project's params code was not imported, and was read only afterwards, to explain the match)

- Schroeder backward integration: `S(t_k) = Σ_{j≥k} E_j` at the step edges `t_k = k·dt`, which are the
  points where a step-constant series integrates exactly. The level is `L = 10 lg(S/S(0))`.
- **Primary fit:** linear least squares over every edge with −35 ≤ L ≤ −5 dB, and T30 = −60/slope.
  Each gated value used 96 to 1,366 points.
- Sensitivity variants (not used for the verdict):
  - (b) step centres;
  - (c) a continuous least-squares fit of the piecewise-linear curve between its exact −5 and −35 dB
    crossings;
  - (d) ISO's truncation compensation: an exponential tail from the last 10 steps.
- Checks as in SPEC §5.1:
  - A: `x_s = mean_r T/T_K − 1` per band, with a t interval (t = 2.2622, 9 degrees of freedom).
  - B: `(max − min)/mean` of `m_s = mean_{r,b} T/T_K`.
  - C: `y_s = mean_{r,b} T/T_tr − 1`, with `SE² = var(y)/10 + se_tr²`, where `se_tr` is the mean over
    bands of `transport_se/transport_t`. The statistic is `|d| + t·SE`.

  discretisation. The per-cell mean is at most 0.0015 %, and the sign varies.

## 5. Side checks

- **dt, seeds and matrix, from the files the runs actually used.** Every one of the 400 runs has:
  - `pasdetemps="0.001"` in `solve/config.xml`;
  - the `.gap` time step equal to 1.00000005e-3 (f32);
  - `.recp` rows labelled from `1.0 ms` and numbering exactly duration/dt.

  Every cell has:
  - seeds 1 to 10, each with `random_seed` equal to its folder;
  - the three receivers R000 to R002 at SPEC §2.1's positions, and the source at SPEC's position;
  - 6 bands with air off and 7 with air on (125 Hz to 4 or 8 kHz);
  - particles, duration and `trans_epsilon` as in SPEC §2.3, which I transcribed from the SPEC and
    not from `beds/m8a.json`;
  - uniform α, scattering 1 and Lambert reflection (`loi` 2);
  - `computation_method` 0 for random and 1 for energetic.

  0 problems. Within every cell, the ten seeds' `.recp` files all differ (sha256), so no seed repeats
  another.
- **Truncation.** The energy an exponential tail would add past the end of the series is at most
  2.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ of the total in energetic cells, and 0 in random cells, where every particle has been
  absorbed. With ISO's compensation (variant d), every A, B and C statistic is unchanged to four
  decimals of a percent.
- **Kuttruff, recomputed** from the box's V and S, SPEC's formula with c = 343.2, and ISO 9613-1 for
  the air term:
  - air off, with the report's γ²: within 5 × 10⁻⁸ of `kuttruff_s`;
  - with SPEC's exact box γ²: within 2.9 × 10⁻⁵;
  - air on: within 2.05 × 10⁻⁴. My ISO 9613-1 `m` is 0.033 % from the solver's form, inside SPEC's
    0.035 %.

  Rerunning A and B against my own Kuttruff changes no verdict. The worst A edge moves from 1.1784 % to
  1.1777 %, in 5x4x3-a0.1-random-air-on.

## 6. The refused 20×8×4 m random cells (reported only)

- **The refusals match the raw data.**
  - α 0.4, `range_not_reached`, 26 values, all at R002: in each of the 26, the last step holding
    energy lies 32.3 to 35.0 dB below the total, so the curve drops to nothing within a single step
    before reaching −35 dB. Every value that was not refused has its last energy step at −35.0 dB or
    lower. The threshold separates the two groups exactly.
  - α 0.05 and 0.1: the 3 + 3 `missing_moves` values are each in a single receiver-band of a single
    seed.
- **A plain ISO Schroeder fit has no guard for a sparse tail, and returns values for all of them.** If
  those values were used:

  | Cell | A worst edge | B | C: d [95 % interval] |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | α 0.05 | 2.46 % | 1.61 % | +0.20 % [−0.15, +0.55]: inconclusive |
  | α 0.1 | 3.60 % | 2.63 % (fails 2 %) | +0.41 % [−0.21, +1.03] |
  | α 0.4 | 9.13 % (fails 5 %) | 3.92 % | +2.24 % [+1.22, +3.27]: excludes 0, so it would **fail** |

  These values are built on a handful of late particles. They show that the refusals are right, and
  they add to RUN.md's finding: random mode at 1.5M particles in a 640 m³ room is unusable. They do not
  affect the pass, since the room is not gated.

## 7. What this does not show

- It does not test whether ISO T30 is biased when read from a Monte Carlo decay. My method and the
  bed's agree, so they would share any such bias. Only check C, against the transport, speaks to it,
  and I did not recompute the transport: `transport_t`, `transport_se` and `gamma2` come from
  `report.json`.
- It covers only boxes with uniform α, Lambert walls with scattering 1, and dt 1 ms. It says nothing
  about specular or partly diffuse walls, uneven absorption, other steps, or EDT and T20 (M8b).
- Gated random counts are 3.5 to 110 million particles, 23 to 730 times the 150,000 a user runs. The
  pass says nothing about T30 at the counts the app ships with.
- I verified that the ten seeds give distinct outputs. I did not verify that they are statistically
  independent draws, which is what B and C's intervals assume.
